EU asset-management regulation is moving through implementation rather than wholesale redesign. Managers are absorbing concrete requirements for liquidity, private credit, delegation, reporting, and risk governance while selected product and disclosure rules move in the opposite direction: toward fewer barriers and simpler information.

That tension matters. Calling every development "more regulation" misses the liberalization in ELTIF 2.0 and the European Commission's proposed simplification of SFDR. Calling the agenda "simplification" misses the operating-model work already attached to AIFMD II, UCITS, and ESMA supervision.

The practical outlook is a two-track regime: **build stronger control evidence while preparing for selective simplification.**

## Read the Status Before the Headline

The developments below do not all have the same legal weight. Keeping their status visible prevents a proposal or supervisory exercise from being treated as enacted law.

| Development | Status on 23 August 2026 | What the status means |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ELTIF 2.0 | Binding EU regulation in force | The amended regime has applied since 10 January 2024. |
| AIFMD II and UCITS amendments | EU directive implemented through national law | Member States were required to apply most transposing measures from 16 April 2026; national choices may differ. |
| ESMA fund-cost figures | Observed market evidence | The figures describe 2024 outcomes; they are not a new rule. |
| SFDR revision | European Commission proposal | The November 2025 proposal is not enacted law and may change during negotiation. |
| ESMA risk-management review | Supervisory activity | Authorities are examining firms during 2026 and 2027; findings are not expected until 2028. |

This is an EU-level view. It does not compare Member-State transposition, and it excludes the United Kingdom and Switzerland.

## 1. Liquidity and Private Credit Become Operating-Model Concerns

[Directive (EU) 2024/927](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2024/927/oj/eng), commonly called AIFMD II, aligns parts of the AIFMD and UCITS frameworks while adding a common regime for loan origination by alternative investment funds.

For open-ended AIFs and UCITS, the directive requires managers to select at least two suitable liquidity-management tools from a harmonized list, subject to limited exceptions for money market funds. The list includes redemption gates, notice-period extensions, redemption fees, swing pricing, dual pricing, anti-dilution levies, and redemptions in kind. Suspensions and side pockets remain available for exceptional circumstances.

Selection is only the visible part of the work. A manager needs to connect each tool to the fund's investment strategy, liquidity profile, and redemption policy; disclose the conditions for use; establish activation governance; and demonstrate fair treatment of investors.

Private-credit managers face an equally operational set of changes. The directive recognizes loan origination as an AIF activity while requiring proportionate credit-granting, credit-risk assessment, administration, and portfolio-monitoring processes. It also introduces safeguards covering leverage, conflicts, risk retention, originate-to-distribute strategies, and the conditions under which a loan-originating AIF may remain open-ended.

Delegation becomes more data-intensive too. Managers must provide supervisors with information about delegates, delegated activities, affected assets, oversight, sub-delegation, and the duration of arrangements. The directive also requires ESMA guidance on when a UCITS name is unfair, unclear, or misleading, making naming governance part of the same evidence problem rather than a separate marketing exercise.

Member States were required to apply most transposing measures from **16 April 2026**. New UCITS supervisory-reporting measures apply from **16 April 2027**. Firms still need to check the law and supervisory practice in each relevant Member State rather than assume identical implementation.

## 2. ELTIF 2.0 Broadens the Product Opportunity

[Regulation (EU) 2023/606](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2023/606/oj/eng) has applied since 10 January 2024. It changes the European Long-Term Investment Fund regime in ways intended to make the product more usable for managers and investors.

The amended rules lower the minimum allocation to eligible investment assets from 70% to 55%. They broaden eligible real assets and fund-of-funds strategies, permit master-feeder structures, relax some diversification limits, and allow more flexible borrowing and redemption arrangements.

For retail distribution, ELTIF 2.0 removes the previous EUR 10,000 initial minimum and the 10% aggregate portfolio cap for investors whose financial-instrument portfolios did not exceed EUR 500,000. MiFID suitability and product-governance requirements continue to matter; removal of those numerical barriers is not removal of investor protection.

These changes create product-design options. They do **not** prove that ELTIF assets, fundraising, or retail adoption subsequently increased. The regulation establishes the opportunity, not the market outcome.

## 3. Fee Pressure Comes Mainly From Product Turnover

ESMA's [2025 market report on the costs and performance of EU retail investment products](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/new-investment-funds-drive-reduction-costs-investors), published in March 2026 and reporting 2024 outcomes, shows gradual cost compression with an important qualification.

ESMA reports that ongoing costs fell by 8% for retail equity funds and by almost 15% for retail bond funds. Among existing funds, the declines were smaller: 3% for equity funds and 9% for bond funds. ESMA attributes much of the overall reduction to lower-priced new funds entering the market rather than incumbent funds repricing at the same rate.

The same report says real net returns were positive across UCITS categories in 2024, while ESG UCITS underperformed non-ESG equivalents. It also reports that the retail-investor share of AIF investment fell from 14% in 2022 to 9% in 2024.

The managerial implication is narrower than "fees are falling." Product committees need to compare new and legacy share classes, distribution economics, and investor outcomes. A market average can improve while expensive incumbent products remain exposed to value scrutiny.

## 4. SFDR Moves Toward More Usable Categories

In November 2025, the European Commission [proposed amendments to SFDR](https://finance.ec.europa.eu/publications/commission-simplifies-transparency-rules-sustainable-financial-products_en). The Commission's review found that disclosures had become too long and complex and that SFDR was being used as a de facto labeling system, increasing confusion as well as greenwashing and mis-selling risk.

The proposal seeks simpler, more comparable investor information and lower disclosure costs for financial-product providers. Its direction is relevant to product and sustainability teams, but its provisions must remain in the future tense: the proposal is not current law, and legislative negotiations may alter or reject parts of it.

That creates a difficult transition discipline. Managers should maintain compliance with the current regime, keep sustainability claims consistent across pre-contractual disclosures and marketing, and preserve the data lineage behind those claims without hard-coding an operating model around an unfinalized category system.

## 5. Supervision Tests Risk Governance, Not Only Documentation

ESMA and national competent authorities launched a [common supervisory action on risk-management functions](https://www.esma.europa.eu/press-news/esma-news/esma-launches-common-supervisory-action-ncas-risk-management-function) across UCITS managers and AIFMs for 2026 and 2027.

The exercise focuses on three areas:

1. Governance and organization of the risk-management function.
2. Identification, measurement, and monitoring of material risks.
3. Reporting to senior management and governing bodies.

ESMA specifically frames effectiveness, independence, and expertise as areas of attention. A policy library alone cannot demonstrate those qualities. Firms need evidence that risk limits influence decisions, challenge is independent, escalation works, governing bodies receive decision-useful reporting, and weaknesses are remediated.

ESMA expects to publish the final report in 2028. Until then, the exercise defines supervisory focus, not industry findings.

## The Counter-Position: Simplification Is Real but Narrower

The evidence does not support a one-way story of ever-increasing regulation. ELTIF 2.0 deliberately removes product and retail-distribution barriers. The SFDR proposal explicitly targets complexity and compliance cost. AIFMD II and UCITS reporting work also aims to remove duplication and improve reuse of supervisory data.

But timing matters. ELTIF flexibility is available now, while its adoption effects remain unproven. SFDR simplification is prospective. Reporting efficiencies depend on technical implementation. By contrast, liquidity, loan-origination, delegation, and governance controls already demand operating-model evidence.

For 2026 and 2027, the concrete build-out is therefore broader than the realized simplification benefit.

## A Readiness Matrix for Managers

The useful question is not whether a regulation belongs to legal, risk, product, or operations. It is whether the firm can produce consistent evidence across all four.

<div className="overflow-x-auto">

| Function | Priority | Evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Board and risk governance | Test independence, expertise, escalation, and challenge | Committee packs, limit decisions, challenge records, remediation tracking |
| Portfolio and liquidity management | Select and calibrate tools against fund characteristics | Liquidity assessments, activation playbooks, investor disclosures, test results |
| Private credit underwriting | Align origination and monitoring with AIFMD II safeguards | Credit policies, approvals, portfolio monitoring, conflicts and retention controls |
| Product, legal, and distribution | Reassess ELTIF design and fund-name governance | Product approvals, suitability controls, naming rationale, disclosure consistency |
| Sustainability | Maintain current SFDR controls while tracking the proposal | Claim and data lineage, review records, marketing and disclosure reconciliation |
| Operations and data | Prepare delegation and UCITS reporting data | Ownership maps, delegate inventories, data definitions, quality and lineage checks |
| Compliance | Track EU rules through national implementation | Jurisdiction inventory, legal mapping, control owners, dated implementation evidence |

</div>

These are readiness priorities, not a substitute for legal advice. The responsible owner should validate obligations against the fund, manager, investor base, and applicable national law.

## The 2026-2027 Outlook

EU asset management is moving on two tracks. Managers must operationalize liquidity, risk, delegation, private-credit, and reporting controls while product and disclosure frameworks are selectively liberalized or simplified.

The firms best positioned for that combination will not treat each rule as an isolated compliance project. They will reuse fund data, control ownership, governance evidence, and disclosure lineage across regimes. That reduces duplicated implementation without pretending the underlying obligations are identical.

Claims that these reforms will materially increase EU retail participation, ELTIF scale, cross-border competition, or sustainable capital allocation remain ahead of the evidence. For now, the defensible conclusion is simpler: the control build-out is real, the simplification is selective, and managers need to distinguish enacted requirements from expected outcomes.
